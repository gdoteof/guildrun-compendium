How this works

Where the data comes from

Guildrun writes structured logs next to the game (Guildrun_Data/Logs; on macOS Guildrun.app/Contents/Logs), and every battle logs its complete starting state — party, ranks, items, relics, shard balance, RNG seed. Diffing consecutive battles reconstructs what happened in each shop phase, even though purchases are never logged directly. Players contribute by dropping their log folder on the home page; nothing is installed and the game is never touched.

Rankings

Guildrun is PvE, so there is no head-to-head Elo. Instead, per context (game version × difficulty × floor band), each entity gets an empirical-Bayes shrunk success rate:

score = (wins + k·p0) / (n + k)

where p0 is the baseline success rate in that exact context and k a shrinkage weight (20 for battles, 8 for runs). Lift is score ÷ baseline; tiers are fixed lift bands (S ≥ 1.10, A ≥ 1.03, B ≥ 0.97, C ≥ 0.90, else D). Small samples are pulled toward 1.00× — an entity seen twice cannot rocket to S tier off a lucky pair of wins. Entries with fewer than 5 runs are flagged.

Two outcome families are tracked: battle-level (did this battle end in victory with the entity fielded?) and run-level (was a run that ever used the entity beaten?). Tier assignment prefers run-level lift when the sample allows.

Honest limits

  • Skill confounding: pick rate correlates with player skill; a popular pick among strong players inflates. Per-player segmentation (anonymous) exists in the data model to correct for this as the sample grows.
  • Shop offers are counted, not named: the game logs how many things were offered vs. bought, but names only items on sale — so "win rate when offered but skipped" is only computable for sale offers.
  • Prices aren't recoverable: shard movement is only known as a net figure per shop phase.
  • Unequipped items are invisible: an item bought but never equipped for a battle never appears in any log line.

Privacy

Log files contain your SteamID in a few file-path lines. At ingest the ID is used once to derive an anonymous identity (HMAC with a server-side secret), then scrubbed from the raw text before anything is stored. No raw SteamID is stored or served, and nothing else in the logs is personal. Signing in through Steam verifies your identity with Steam's OpenID service and stores only your public persona name against that same anonymous identity — which is how your previously uploaded runs become "yours" automatically.
